# DealerPulse: what to say

Target about 10 minutes. Let him interrupt, that is a good sign. Answers to the
likely questions are at the bottom.

## 0. The whole thing in 30 seconds

Say this first so he knows where you are going.

"You gave me a raw file of 510 enquiries across five showrooms. I found that
one branch, Lakeside, converts at 7.6 percent where the group does 31.4, and
the break is right at the top, 42 percent of its leads are never phoned. So I
built the dashboard to open with that one sentence instead of a wall of charts,
and everything under it is there so you can check whether I am right."

Then slow down and take it from the start.

## 1. The business, in his words (30 seconds)

Do not skip this. It shows you understand the shop floor, not just the file.

"Five showrooms, thirty sales people, seven models. A lead is one person who
raised their hand. They walk in, or fill a form, or DM you on Instagram. Then
they move through six steps. New, contacted, test drive, negotiation, order
placed, delivered. They can drop out at any one of them.

A lead is not a sale. A lead is a chance at one. So the whole business is how
many of those chances become cars, and where you lose the rest."

## 2. What I was actually given (30 seconds)

"Half a megabyte of raw records. 510 leads, June to December. 160 deliveries.
No dashboard, no report, and no question.

That last part is the interesting bit. Nobody told me what to look for. So the
first job was working out what the question even was."

Pause. Then:

"And I decided the question is not can we see our numbers. It is nobody can see
where the money is leaking, and by the time they can, the leads are cold. A
branch manager only sees his own branch and thinks he is fine. The group average
hides the one showroom dropping four out of five enquiries on the floor."

## 3. The screen tells you, you do not have to hunt (1 minute)

Open the overview and read the top line off the screen.

> Lakeside converts 7.6 percent of its leads where the group converts 31.4
> percent, and the break is at the very first step. 42 percent of Lakeside
> leads are never called.

"I did not type that sentence. The code compares every branch, finds the one
furthest behind its peers, walks its funnel step by step, works out which step
bleeds the most, and writes the line from what it finds. Different data next
month gives a different line, or it tells you there is nothing worth saying."

Then the point that lands:

"And notice what the finding is. Not Lakeside is underperforming, you already
suspect that. It is that they are losing people they never spoke to. You cannot
lose a deal you never started. That is a habit, and a habit you can fix on a
Monday morning."

## 4. Three screens only (3 minutes)

**Overview.** Point at the two money boxes.
"Fixing the phone calls is worth about 40 to 56 lakh. Fixing the whole funnel is
worth 3.95 to 5.5 crore. I keep them apart because they are very different sized
jobs and mixing them would have oversold the easy one."

Scroll to branch comparison.
"Every rate carries its count. Lakeside is 6 of 79. Sixty percent of five and
sixty percent of five hundred should never look the same on a screen."

**Act now.**
"This is the to do list. 8.59 crore is sitting in 38 orders where the customer
already paid and no car was delivered. Normal is 17 days. The oldest is 195. And
30 of those 38 are already past the date your own rep promised the customer."

Tap "Why this order?"
"The ranking is a formula I show on screen. Money already committed outranks
money still being discussed."

**Vehicles.**
"Glanza pulls 130 enquiries and earns 3.97 crore. Fortuner pulls 94 and earns
12.61. Where the floor spends its time is a choice, not an accident."

## 5. Three judgement calls (2 minutes)

These are what separate you from someone who just made charts.

**Money is a range, not one number.**
"Those 38 stalled orders. If your team chases them most complete. If nobody
does, many will not. I genuinely do not know which, so I show both ends and say
what separates them, instead of inventing one confident figure."

**Sometimes it refuses to answer.**
"Under ten leads it will not print a rate, it prints a dash. Under thirty it
will not name a branch as your problem. A rate on four leads looks identical to
a rate on four hundred once you put a percent sign on it. That is how dashboards
mislead people without ever lying."

**A quarter has two right answers.**
Switch the basis button on Q4.
"24.81 crore is what you delivered in those three months. 17.55 crore is how the
leads you took in those months converted, because some of them will not deliver
until March. Both correct. Completely different conversations. Most dashboards
silently pick one and never tell you. I let you switch, and the label always
says which one you are looking at."

## 6. What I chose not to build (30 seconds)

Say this. It matters more than the feature list.

"I dropped a row spacing control after I measured it. It saved twelve percent of
height and added a button that looked like a menu. I skipped a what changed
since last visit marker, because your data is a fixed export and it would have
been theatre."

## 7. The next feature, and why it is the right one (2 minutes)

This is your strongest two minutes. Do not rush it.

Set up the problem first.

"Here is what happens after my dashboard does its job. It tells you Lakeside is
the problem. You pick up the phone. And the branch manager has three answers
ready before you finish the sentence. Our leads come from worse sources. We sell
cheaper cars. We are short staffed.

Right now you have a number and no answer to any of that. So the call goes
nowhere and you both agree to look at it next month."

Then the idea.

"So the next thing I build is the product arguing against its own finding,
before you walk into the room. Every excuse a branch manager could give, checked
automatically, in plain language."

Give three, not five. Three is enough and it stays memorable.

"Worse leads. I take Lakeside's own source mix and price it at what the other
four branches convert. It predicts 34.8 percent. They deliver 7.6.

Cheaper cars. Their own model mix predicts 36 percent. And their average enquiry
is actually slightly bigger than the group, 24.8 lakh against 24.1.

Short staffed. They carry the lightest load in the group, 15.8 leads per sales
person. Eastside carries 25.4 and converts 37 percent."

Then the line that decides it:

"And the chance of a gap this size being luck is about one in 1.4 million. They
would need 23 more cars out of the same 79 leads to reach where their peers are."

Now the part that shows judgement, not just cleverness:

"The important thing is it cuts both ways. I ran the same five checks on all
five branches, not just Lakeside. Four of them get cleared. Highway is at 33
against a mix that predicts 31, so there is no case to answer and the product
says so. Downtown is at 41 against a mix predicting 30, so it flags them as
worth copying, not fixing. Only Lakeside fails.

A dashboard that can only ever agree with itself is not handling uncertainty,
it is hiding it. This one tries to prove itself wrong first and reports what
survived."

If he asks whether existing tools do this, be straight:

"The statistics are ordinary, they are about a hundred years old. Power BI and
ThoughtSpot both have features that find likely drivers. What none of them do is
take the specific objections your branch manager will actually say out loud and
answer them in his language, then clear him when he is right. That framing is
the new part, not the maths."

And close it on cost:

"It runs entirely on data you already have in the file. No new pipeline, no new
integration. It is about a day of work."

## 8. Close (20 seconds)

"Three things after that. Point it at a live source and unfreeze the clock. Let
people share saved views with each other. And sit with one branch manager for an
hour to see which screen he opens second. I have a guess. I would rather know."

## If he asks

**Why is the clock frozen at 31 December?**
"Your data ends there, so the product treats that as today. Otherwise every age
and every overdue flag drifts a little further from the truth every day, and a
link you shared in January shows different numbers in March."

**How do you know the numbers are right?**
"33 checks recompute every headline figure straight from your raw file and
compare it to what the app shows. Different code on each side, so a bug that
moves both the same way still fails."

**How do you know it works on a phone?**
"A second suite opens a real browser over 32 views at four widths in both
themes. It caught a two pixel decoration I had added that was pushing every
table row one column across from its own header. Invisible on a laptop. On a
phone it put the customer name over the value column."

**Biggest weakness?**
"It reads a file, not a live system. And your target data is unusable. The plan
asks for 1,426 units when only 510 leads exist, so I show attainment for
continuity and keep it out of every health signal."

**How long did it take?** Give your real number.

**Did you use AI?**
Be straight. "Yes, like most engineers now. The judgement calls, the checks and
the things I removed are mine, and I can walk you through any line of it."
Do not deny it. Getting caught on that is worse than the question.
