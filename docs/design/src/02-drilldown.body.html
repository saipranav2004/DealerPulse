DealerPulse · Design System 02 of 03

Diagnosis: branch, rep, lead.

The Overview states a conclusion. These three screens exist to prove or disprove it fast enough that a manager does not need an analyst. Same tokens, same components, one new one: the benchmark funnel.

01

Navigation and carried state

Three levels, one back path. Filters set on the Overview follow the user down — silently dropping them would make branch figures disagree with the screen the user just left, which is how people stop trusting a tool. Carried filters appear as the same active chips, plus a line stating what is inherited.

Target attainment

Lakeside was given a 264-unit target for seven months and received 79 leads. Even converting every single lead the branch could have delivered 79 — 30% of its target. The gap between 79 and 264 is a lead-supply question for the group, not a performance question for this branch. Attainment is shown for continuity and drives no health signal here.

1

The break step is set at 26px against 13px everywhere else. It is twice the size of its neighbours, sits on a shifted ground, and is fenced by a 2px rule above. Remove all colour and it still reads first — which was the requirement.

2

Two funnels, not one. The pale bar is the same 79 leads at group rates. The widening white space between pale and solid is the loss, rendered as area rather than asserted in a caption.

3

Conversions sit between stages, not on them. A conversion is a transition; putting the number on the stage row is the most common funnel error and it makes deltas ambiguous.

4

"Systemic" is proved by a distribution, not asserted. Five accent ticks at the far left of 25, with a dashed 16-point void before the sixth. No sentence could make that point as fast.

5

The manager row is present but unscored. Showing Rahul Patel with a dash rather than 0% avoids ranking him last on a metric he has no way to move.

6

Cycle time exists to rule something out. It shows Lakeside is only 1–2 days slow everywhere, which is what licenses the conclusion that speed is not the problem.

Reflow decisions

1

The funnel never degrades. It is the reason to open this screen, so it takes full width and keeps both bars. The benchmark caption folds under the percentage instead of sitting beside it.

2

Header goes single-column; win rate and revenue become an inline pair under a hairline. The manager finding keeps full prominence — it is a finding, not a decoration.

Table strategy: scroll, not cards

3

Horizontal scroll with a pinned name column, chosen over card transformation. The entire job of this table is comparing eight numbers across reps; cards stack reps vertically and destroy exactly that comparison. Pinning the name keeps every scrolled figure attributable.

4

Removed at 768: the distribution strip and the "Open" column. The strip needs horizontal room to show its gap and would mislead when squeezed; it is replaced by a tap-through. Cycle time and targets move below the fold.

No leads are assigned to Rahul Patel

Branch managers at all five branches carry zero pipeline, so there is no funnel, win rate or revenue to show here. That is a reporting structure, not a gap in the data.

What we can still say: Rahul manages five officers holding 79 leads, of which 33 were never contacted. Manager accountability on this screen is expressed through the branch, because that is where the data actually attaches.

1

Percentile without a rank number. Three stacked rails — rep, branch, company median — say exactly where he stands without printing "25th of 25", which is the phrasing that turns a review into a dismissal.

2

One controllable metric is promoted. Win rate depends on lead quality, branch process and luck. "12 of 22 never contacted" depends on him. Making that the largest number on the page is what makes the screen usable in a coaching conversation.

3

The empty state states a finding, not an absence. It refuses to render zeros that would read as failure, and it redirects to the level where the manager's work is visible.

4

Low-n propagates. Below contacted this rep's stages fall under 10, so the funnel shows counts and withholds rates — the same guard as everywhere else.

The wrongness is drawn, not labelled. Every normal gap is a one-line hairline chip; the 195-day gap becomes a block with 19px numerals and a bar whose black tick shows where delivery should have finished. The timeline itself deforms.

08

Interaction model

GestureMeaningRule
ClickChange scopeAlways navigates one level down, or opens the lead drawer. A click never sorts, toggles or filters. Branch row → branch screen; rep row → rep screen; lead row → drawer.
HoverAdd precisionReveals denominators, money and medians behind a number already shown. Never carries information needed to read the chart, because tablets have no hover — every hover fact is also reachable by tap.
FilterChange the populationApplies to the whole screen, persists across navigation, and is always restated in the subset bar. Filters never change what a metric means, only which rows feed it.
SortReorder, nothing elseColumn headers only. Sorting never re-colours rows: the focus entity keeps its accent wherever it lands.
